Settings

Settings is where you choose how Yen Studio looks, where your songs are saved, and where its sound goes. It opens as a window over the app, and you can keep working with it open.

Press ⌘, to open it, or choose Settings… from the Edit menu. Press ⌘, again or Esc to close it. With a song open it starts on the Music tab; from the Home screen it starts on General. Click a tab to switch, or use the arrow keys, Home and End on the tabs.

General

How the whole app looks, and where new songs go.

Save new projects in

Each song is a file of its own, a .yen package. You can move, copy or back it up in Finder like any other file.

Save new projects in shows the folder new songs are saved to. It starts as Documents/Yen Studio in your home folder. Click Change… to pick another folder. Once you've changed it, Reset puts it back.

Songs you already have stay where they are: this only changes where new ones go.

App icon

Choose how the app's icon looks in the Dock and in Finder. Click one of the icons shown.

Show tabs

Under Window, Show tabs turns the strip of open songs along the top of the window on or off. Without it, the menus start the window. ⌘P still finds and switches between your songs.

Fonts

Under Text:

  • Interface font: menus, panels, labels and the assistant's chat. DM Sans by default; Inter, Geist, IBM Plex Sans, Figtree, Manrope, Tahoma, or your system's font.
  • Numbers and code: times, values, the position and tempo displays. Geist Mono by default; JetBrains Mono, IBM Plex Mono, Fira Code, or your system's monospaced font.

A sample line under them shows both.

Send usage and crash reports

Under Reports, Send usage and crash reports is off until you say yes. The first time Yen Studio opens, a card in the corner asks Help make Yen Studio better? Click Send reports or No thanks. Either way, you can change it here at any time.

When it's on, reports say:

  • your version of Yen Studio and your system;
  • when the app opens, plays and exports;
  • which plugins you load;
  • what went wrong when something breaks, including a crash read from macOS's crash reports the next time the app opens.

They never include your songs, sounds or file names, and nothing tied to your name. Nothing is sent before you say yes, and nothing is sent once it's off.

Logs

Logs keeps a record of what the app did and what went wrong, on your computer only. Click Show in Finder next to Log files to find them. Only the latest few megabytes are kept. See Problems and logs for when to use them.

Music

Where your sound goes, how sound is read, and your plugins.

Audio device

The engine plays every song through these devices, whichever song is open.

  • Output device: where you hear the song. If the device has its own control panel, a Device panel button opens it.
  • Input device: what you record from. Choose No input if you don't record audio. To record you need an audio interface, or the computer's microphone. Use headphones with a microphone, so the song isn't recorded too. Once an input is chosen, the line under it says how many inputs it has and how late it is.

Choosing an input asks macOS for access to the microphone first. If you said no, allow Yen Studio in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone, then choose the input again.

Note: Until you choose an output device here, the sound follows your Mac's own output, so it moves to headphones when you plug them in. Once you choose one, Yen Studio keeps to it. See Where the sound goes.

Buffer length

Buffer length is the size of the engine's audio buffer, in samples. The line under it says how many milliseconds of buffer that is, and how long until a sound is heard on this device.

A smaller buffer answers faster, which matters when you play a MIDI keyboard or record. A larger one works the computer less hard. If you hear clicks or dropouts, raise it.

Sample rate

Sample rate is the rate the engine runs at. 44.1 or 48 kHz suits most work. Higher rates work the computer harder.

Recording offset

Recording offset shows when your device reports it. Takes already land where the driver's latency says they should. If your takes still sit late, set this to move them earlier by that many milliseconds. A negative number moves them later. See Recording audio.

MIDI inputs

Each MIDI keyboard or pad plugged in is listed, with a Use switch. Keyboards play the channel selected in the Channel rack, and recording keeps what you play. A keyboard you plug in is used at once: you don't need to come back here.

Under each keyboard a line says what it's doing:

  • Playing the selected channel.
  • Couldn't be opened: another app may have it. Quit the other app, or turn the keyboard off there.
  • Turned off.

With no keyboard, ⌘T plays the selected channel from your computer's keyboard instead. See MIDI keyboards.

Quality

How samplers and audio clips are read when they're pitched, or recorded at another rate than the song. Better methods sound cleaner (no faint whistles on bright sounds) and cost more. Your plugins aren't affected.

  • Resampling while playing: Linear, Hermite (4-point) or Sinc (32-point).
  • Resampling in exports: the same choices, used when you export.
  • Stretch mode: how an audio clip stretches when you drag its edge with the Playlist's Stretch on (⇧M). Stretch keeps its pitch. Resample makes it higher when it's faster, as a record would.

Reset plugins on stop

Under Playback. On, stopping silences delays, reverbs and every other tail at once. Off, they ring out after a stop, and pressing Stop again silences them.

Open favourite folders in place

Under Browser. On, a folder you've starred opens under Favourites in the Browser. Off, it's a shortcut: clicking it shows the folder where it is among your folders.

Engine and plugins

The state of the audio engine, and a test bench for your plugins.

The status card shows the device, its sample rate and buffer, how late its output is, how hard the engine is working, any dropouts, and the output's level on a meter. Turn on Test tone to check you can hear the engine at all.

The Plugins card says how many plugins were found (VST3 and Audio Units). Click Scan to look for plugins you've installed since. A plugin that crashed during the scan is listed under it as skipped, with the reason and a way to try it again. See Plugins.

The Instrument and Effect cards try a plugin on its own, outside any song:

  1. Pick an instrument under Instrument. Play it with the keys under the cards.
  2. Pick an effect under Effect. The song, the instrument and the test tone all go through it.
  3. Use Open window to see its own window, Save state and Restore to keep and bring back its settings, and Remove to take it off the bench.

Starting point

Under New songs. New songs start blank. To start each one from a song of yours, choose Use as starting point in its menu on the Home screen. Once one is set, Start blank here goes back to blank songs.
