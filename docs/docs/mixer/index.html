Mix

Mixer

The Mixer is where every channel's sound ends up. Each channel in the Channel rack plays into a mixer track; each track has a fader, pan, effect slots and an output, and every track ends in the master. It works the way FL Studio's mixer does: route channels with ⌘L, send tracks to each other with the switch under each strip, and put effects in the slots on the right.

Open the Mixer

Press F9 (or ⌥4), or use the Mixer button on the toolbar. It opens filling the window. Double-click its title bar to make it a window you can move and resize, and double-click again to fill the window.

The Mixer has three parts:

  • The strips: the master first (marked M), then the tracks. Scroll along them with the mouse wheel.
  • The inspector on the right: the current track's name, its input, its effect slots and its delay compensation.
  • The title bar: the Mixer menu (the arrow), the SOLO light, MONO, DIM, the view, and + to add a track.

Tracks

Add and delete tracks

  • Click + in the title bar to add a track after the selected one, or the + after the last strip to add one at the end.
  • Right-click a track's number or name and choose Insert a track after.
  • To delete, select the tracks and press ⌥⌫, or choose Delete from the track's menu or Delete selected tracks from the Mixer menu.

The master can't be deleted or moved.

Select tracks

  • Click a strip to select it. It becomes the current track, the one the inspector shows.
  • ⌘-click adds a track to the selection. ⇧-click selects every track from the current one to the one you click.
  • Drag across the names and meters to move the selection along. Hold ⌘ while you drag to add every track you pass over.
  • ← and → select the previous and next track. ⌘A selects them all.
  • Esc, or a click on empty space, keeps only the current track selected.

A control you move on a selected strip moves on every selected track: faders move together by the same number of decibels, pan and stereo separation by the same amount, and mute, solo, invert and swap all take the state of the strip you clicked.

Rename, colour and reorder

  • Double-click a track's name, press F2 or ⇧⌘N, or choose Rename and color… from its menu. Clear the name to go back to the track's number.
  • Colour ▸ in the track's menu picks a colour from a set of swatches, or No colour. A coloured track's strip is tinted.
  • Drag a track's number to move it (the selected tracks go with it). A line shows where they'll land. You can also press ⌥← and ⌥→, or choose Move left and Move right.

Route channels to the Mixer

A channel plays into the mixer track its settings name. To route several at once:

  1. Select the channels in the Channel rack.
  2. Select a mixer track.
  3. Press ⌘L (Route selected channels to this track) to send them all there, or ⇧⌘L (…starting from this track) to give each channel a track of its own, one after the other.

Both are in the Mixer menu and under Channel routing ▸ in a track's menu. ⌥L (Select channels routed here) selects, in the Channel rack, every channel that plays into the current track.

Faders, pan and stereo

Fader. Drag the handle, or press anywhere in the groove to bring the handle there and keep dragging. It catches at 0 dB on the way past; hold ⇧ to move through it. Hold ⌘ for fine changes. The mouse wheel moves it too. ⌥-click (or a middle click) puts it back at 0 dB. Right-click for Reset, Type in value… (in dB) and the automation items.

Pan and stereo separation are knobs: drag up or down, ⌥-click to reset, right-click for Reset, Copy value, Paste value and Type in value…. Stereo separation reads As it is in the middle; turned right it makes the sound narrower, turned left wider.

Invert phase flips the track's polarity. Swap left/right swaps its two sides.

Every control on a strip can be automated and modulated: right-click it.

Mute and solo

  • Click the mute light to mute a track. Keep the button down and drag across other strips to mute them too.
  • Click S to solo a track (drag across strips to solo several). ⌥-click S, press ⌥S, or ⌘-click or right-click the mute light to solo a track alone.
  • ⇧-click S to make a track solo safe (a lock shows): it's never silenced by another track's solo. Useful for a reverb bus. Solo safe is also in the track's menu.
  • The SOLO light in the title bar blinks while anything is soloed, with a count when more than one is. Click it to clear every solo (or choose Clear all solos).

Soloing a track keeps what it plays through: the buses it sends into stay on, and their S is outlined.

A muted or soloed-out strip is greyed. A track that's silent because of something further along (it sends into a muted bus, or sends nowhere) is faded a little less; hover over it to see why.

Routing

Every track goes to the master until you send it somewhere else. Click a strip's output label for its Route to menu: every track it can go to, with a tick by those it goes to now, and New bus.

Sends

The send switch under each strip works on the selected tracks:

  • On a track that isn't selected, the switch is an up arrow. Click it to send the selected tracks there; click again to stop. It's half lit when only some of them send there.
  • Once a track sends to it, the switch shows a knob: the send's level (100% to start). It moves the send of every selected track at once.
  • On a selected track the switch is a down arrow. Drag it onto another strip to route the track there and nowhere else; hold ⌘ or ⌥ as you drop to add a send and keep the routes it has. A cable follows the pointer while you drag.
  • A switch is greyed when sending there would make a loop: that track's sound already reaches the selected ones.

Right-click a send switch for Route to this track (or Remove the sends), Route to this track only, Sidechain to this track (a send at 0%, as in FL Studio) and, once it sends, Pre-fader.

The same choices are under Track routing ▸ in a track's menu, with Route to the master only.

Pre-fader sends

A send is taken after the track's fader, so turning the track down turns the send down too. Tick Pre-fader in the send switch's menu to take it before the fader instead: the send keeps its level whatever the fader does. A PRE badge marks it.

Buses

Select some tracks and press ⌘G (Route selected to a new bus). A new track is added after them, they all go into it, and you name it at once.

A track that others send into shows a fold chip with how many feed it. Click it to fold those tracks away and show only the bus; click again to bring them back.

Cables

The Mixer draws its routing the way FL Studio does. From each selected track, a cable runs from its send to every track it goes to. Into a selected track, dashed cables come from the tracks that feed it. A 0% send is faint, and a sidechain key is dotted, in the accent colour.

Effect slots

The inspector shows the current track's effect slots. An empty one reads Slot and its number.

Add an effect

  1. Click an empty slot.
  2. Pick an effect: the built-in effects come first (Y-EQ, Y-C, Y-L, Y-R, Y-Multiband, Y-Time, Y-Delay, Y-Tune, Y-Pan, Y-Pitch), then the plugins you've starred. More plugins… opens the Plugin manager.

A built-in effect's window opens at once; a plugin's opens when it has loaded. A spinner shows while it loads. If it fails, the slot shows a warning and a button to try again.

Use a slot

  • Click its name to open or close its window.
  • The light on the right turns it on or off (bypass). A bypassed effect's name is struck through.
  • The small knob is its mix, from dry to 100% wet. It can be automated like any knob.
  • A key badge shows when it listens to a sidechain key, with the number of the track it hears.

Click the slot's arrow, or right-click it, for its menu: Open its window, Replace…, Bypass or Enable, Sidechain input ▸, Move up, Move down and Delete.

Reorder, move and copy effects

  • Turn the mouse wheel over a slot to move its effect up or down.
  • Drag a slot onto another slot to swap them. Hold ⌘ to copy it there instead.
  • Drag a slot onto another track's strip to move the effect to that track, with its settings and its automation. Hold ⌘ to copy it. A track with every slot full says No free slot.

Delay compensation

Some effects and plugins delay their sound to do their work. Yen Studio delays every other path to match, so the tracks stay in time. Sends and sidechain keys are compensated too.

At the bottom of the inspector:

  • Plugin delay says how much the track's effects delay it, in milliseconds and samples, or none. Only effects that are switched on count.
  • Offset adds to that, in milliseconds. Use it when a track sounds late (or early) and its plugins don't say so: a positive offset means its sound is later than they report, and the other tracks wait for it. A negative one means it's early. Reset sets it back to 0.

A track whose delay is compensated shows a clock by its number. Hover over it for the plugins' delay and the offset.

Groups and VCA faders

  1. Select the tracks.
  2. Right-click one and choose Create group…, then type its name.

A band with the group's name runs above its tracks. Click the band to select the group's tracks (⌘-click adds them to the selection); double-click it to rename the group. Ungroup in a track's menu takes it out.

  • Link the group's controls: a control moved on one of the group's tracks moves on all of them, whether or not they're selected. A link icon shows on the band.
  • VCA fader for the group: adds a strip after the master, labelled VCA and the group's name. Its fader scales the level of every track in the group without mixing their sound together; each track keeps its own fader and automation. The VCA fader itself can't be automated yet. Its menu has Remove the VCA fader.

Layout

  • Views. The view name in the title bar (Compact, Wide, Extra large) switches to the next one; the Mixer menu lists them too. Extra large adds a list of each strip's effects: click one to open it. A bypassed one is struck through. The view is kept on this computer.
  • Docking. Dock to ▸ Left / Middle / Right in a track's menu pins it. Left-docked tracks stay beside the master and right-docked ones at the far right while the middle scrolls.
  • Separators. Tick Separator before in a track's menu to put a gap before its strip.

Copy track state

Right-click a track and click Copy track state, then choose Paste track state on another. It copies the track's effects with their settings, and its controls. You can also drag Copy track state from the menu onto another strip to copy it there in one go.

Note: a plugin's own settings carry over only within the same song.

Meters

Each strip's meter shows both sides. It's green up to −18 dB, amber towards 0 dB and red over it, and stays red for a moment after the sound goes over.

The SEL meter at the far left adds up the selected tracks' output, with a dB scale: select a few tracks to see how loud they are together.

On the master:

  • MONO sums the master to mono so you can check the mix there.
  • DIM turns the master down by 20 dB so you can talk over it.

Neither changes an export.

Recording

A track that takes an input can be armed and recorded. Its input, monitoring and input offset are at the top of the inspector. Assign to new audio track in a track's menu gives it a Playlist track where its takes land. See Recording audio.

Menus

The Mixer menu (the arrow in the title bar): Add a track, Route selected to a new bus, Delete selected tracks, Modulators… (see Modulators), Route selected channels to this track, Route selected channels starting from this track, and the views.

A track's menu (right-click its number or name):

  • Rename and color…, Colour ▸
  • Channel routing ▸, Track routing ▸, Sidechain to ▸ (see Sidechain)
  • Copy track state, Paste track state
  • Select channels routed here
  • Assign to new audio track
  • Solo, Solo alone, Solo safe, Clear all solos
  • Move left, Move right
  • Separator before
  • Create group…, Ungroup, Link the group's controls, VCA fader for the group
  • Dock to ▸
  • Insert a track after, Delete
