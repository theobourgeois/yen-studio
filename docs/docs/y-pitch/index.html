Mix

Y-Pitch

Y-Pitch moves the pitch of a sound by hand. You turn a wheel, and the sound follows the pointer the way a weight on an elastic would: straight there, or swinging past and back. A second wheel moves the formants, the voice's character, on their own. Notes played on its keyboard, or from a channel of the song, move it too, so one vocal can sing a melody or harmonies. Its settings are saved with the song, every change can be undone, and both wheels can be automated.

Add Y-Pitch to a track

  1. Open the Mixer and pick a track.
  2. Click an empty effect slot. The picker opens.
  3. Under Built in, choose Pitch and formants ("Pitch and formants by hand, swings, harmonies from notes").

The window opens when you click the slot's name. Its title bar names the effect and its track, for example "Y-Pitch (Insert 1)". Close it and the effect keeps playing. Delete the effect and the window closes.

The Mix knob at the window's bottom right sets how much of the shifted sound you hear against the dry sound. It's the same knob as the slot's mix in the Mixer. See Built-in effects for what every built-in effect shares.

The window

  • The top bar: the preset, Resolution and the ⋯ menu.
  • The wheels: Pitch on the left and Timbre on the right, with the Timbre tab between them.
  • The bottom bar: the lights, Midi, the channel its notes come from, Origin and Mix.
  • The keyboard along the bottom.

Turn a wheel

Each wheel is a dial. Its value is in semitones:

  • Drag round it, from anywhere inside it. Hold ⇧ for fine.
  • Scroll over it. A notch is a semitone when it snaps to semitones, an octave when it snaps to octaves, and a tenth of a semitone when it doesn't snap. Hold ⌘ for a hundredth of a semitone.
  • ⌥-click it, or click it with the middle button, to put it back to 0.
  • Double-click it, or press Enter on it, to type a value. What's heard goes straight there, with no swing.
  • The arrow keys step it while it has the focus, ⌘ for fine.

Right-click a wheel for its menu:

  • Type in value…
  • Reset to 0
  • Jump there (no swing): what's heard goes straight to where the pointer is, with no swing on the way.
  • Create automation clip and the other automation items.

The dial shows the pointer, where you've set it, and what's heard as a glowing ring with a trail behind it. When the two don't agree, the sound is still on its way, or swinging.

Range and Snap

The two boxes in a wheel's top corners set how it turns. Click one for its list.

  • Range (top left): how many semitones the whole dial spans, from 2 to 48. 24 semitones (±12) is an octave either way. The Pitch wheel starts at 24, the Timbre wheel at 12.
  • Snap (top right): where the pointer lands. ON is semitones (Semitones in the list), OCT is octaves (Octaves) and OFF is anywhere (Off: anywhere). Both wheels start on semitones.

Gravity, inertia and smooth

Under each wheel, three knobs set how what's heard follows the pointer:

Knob What it does
gravity How hard what's heard is held to the pointer. Low, it swings past and back, as on a weak elastic. High, it's clamped to the pointer.
inertia How slowly what's heard heads for the pointer. You hear it most at low gravity.
smooth How slowly the pointer itself is followed. High, it glides from pitch to pitch, and gravity and inertia matter less.

They start at 75, 45 and 10. Each knob works like every knob in Yen Studio: drag up or down (⌘ for fine), scroll, ⌥-click to reset, and right-click for Type in value…, automation and modulation.

Tip: Set gravity near 0 and smooth to 0, then flick the Pitch wheel and let go: the pitch flies past and swings back. That's the Wild Swing preset.

The Timbre wheel

The Timbre tab between the wheels opens and closes it:

  • On (as it starts): the formants are the Timbre wheel's. At 0 they stay where they were, so a voice moved up or down by the Pitch wheel keeps its character. Turn the Timbre wheel to make the voice smaller and brighter (up) or bigger and darker (down) without changing its notes.
  • Off: the formants move with the pitch, as a tape sped up or slowed down. The Timbre wheel is dimmed.

Play it with notes

Notes move the pitch as well as the wheel. A note above the Origin shifts the sound up by the distance between them: with the origin at C5, playing D5 moves the sound up two semitones. What's heard is the Pitch wheel plus that shift, followed with the same gravity, inertia and smooth.

The keyboard

  • Press a key to play it. Drag along the keys to glide from one to the next.
  • ⇧-click a key to hold it on, until you click it again. Hold several to play a chord in Multi.
  • Right-click a key to make it the origin.

The keyboard shows the origin as a dot and the notes playing as lit keys. In Single it rings the note the sound is at. What you play on it isn't saved in the song.

Notes from

The Notes from list picks a channel of the song whose notes play Y-Pitch as the song plays. Keyboard only, as it starts, takes notes from the keyboard alone. A muted channel says (muted) in the list.

Tip: Draw the melody on a channel, pick it in Notes from, then mute that channel. You hear only the shifted sound, not the instrument the notes are drawn on.

Origin

Origin is the note that shifts nothing. It starts at C5. Drag it up or down or scroll over it, right-click it to type a note (such as "C5", "F#4" or a MIDI note number), or right-click a key on the keyboard.

Single and Multi

  • Single (as it starts): the sound is always heard. The latest note moves it, and it stays there after the note ends, until the next one.
  • Multi: each note plays the sound at its own pitch, so a chord makes harmonies. Without notes, it's silent.

The lights at the bottom left show In when sound is coming in and Midi when notes are playing it.

Resolution

Resolution in the top bar trades how late it is against how clean it sounds:

  • Tight: less late, for playing it live. Notes closer than about 90 Hz blur together.
  • Wide (as it starts): the cleanest. Chords and low notes stay apart, and drums stay sharp. It's later, and Yen Studio makes up for the delay so the track stays in time.

Presets

Pick one from Preset in the top bar. Each says what it does under its name. The name reads Custom once the sound is your own. A preset sets the wheels and the Timbre tab, never Notes from, the origin, Single or Multi, or Resolution.

Preset What it does
Natural Nothing shifted, the knobs as they come: a fresh start
Octave Up An octave higher, the voice's character kept
Octave Down An octave lower, the voice's character kept
Fifth Up Up a fifth, formants kept: a harmony to blend in
Fourth Down Down a fourth, formants kept: a low harmony
Chipmunk Up a fifth with the formants: the tape sped up
Helium The same notes, the formants up: a small, bright voice
Giant Down a fourth, the formants lower still: a big, dark voice
Monster Down an octave with the formants: the tape slowed down
Slow Tape A quarter-tone flat, as a tape running slow
Turntable Nothing snapped, a little weight: play it like a record
Springy Every move overshoots and settles: springy vibrato
Wild Swing No gravity: swing it and the pitch flies past and back for octaves
Slow Glide Notes and moves glide in, as a portamento

The ⋯ menu (Options) has Centre both wheels, and Copy state and Paste state to put every setting on another Y-Pitch.

Automate Y-Pitch

These can be automated: the Pitch and Timbre wheels, and each wheel's gravity, inertia and smooth.

  • Right-click a wheel or a knob and choose Create automation clip.
  • Or touch a control, then click Automate “…” in the window's title bar. It names the last control you touched and makes a clip of it.

See Automation.
